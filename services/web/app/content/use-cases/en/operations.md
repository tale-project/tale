Operational work mixes predictable steps with requests that need investigation and judgment. Tale gives your team a project board for both: people define the work, agents take suitable tasks, and reviewers check the result.

## Give incoming work an owner and a next step

Create tasks for process documentation, supplier research, internal requests, reporting, or preparation for a team handover. Include the relevant references, expected deliverable, and acceptance criteria. Keep proposals in the backlog until the team is ready to commit to them.

Delegate bounded work to configured project agents. They can inspect the permitted information, work in their sandbox, and return a report or files. Comments let teammates clarify the request and ask for changes while the task retains its history.

## Example: improve employee onboarding

| Task | Agent contribution | Team review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect the existing process | Compare supplied checklists and identify gaps | Confirm which steps still apply |
| Draft the onboarding pack | Prepare a guide, schedule, and list of unresolved questions | Check accuracy, ownership, and tone |
| Plan the follow-up | Propose tasks for missing materials or decisions | Assign responsible people and priorities |

Document preparation does not itself provision accounts or grant access. Add those actions only through a separately configured process with the required permissions.

## Use workflows for repeatable steps

When work follows a defined sequence, Tale automations can connect triggers, agent work, and configured connector actions. Agent connector access is read-only through Tale's connector broker; controlled connector writes belong in workflow nodes. Repository tools and explicitly granted secrets have separate permissions.

This lets the team choose the right level of structure: an open task for investigation, or an explicit workflow for a repeatable procedure. Review and approval behavior depends on that configuration.

Tale fits operations teams that want to coordinate people and agents around shared work. A specialist HR, finance, or service-management system can remain the system of record; evaluate its actual connector actions before designing a process around it.
