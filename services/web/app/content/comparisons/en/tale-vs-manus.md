## Are you commissioning an output or coordinating delivery?

An agent that turns a brief into a useful artifact can remove a substantial piece of work. A team project also needs people to agree on the brief, divide responsibilities, and decide whether the output is ready. Compare the whole delivery process when deciding where agent work should happen.

[Manus](https://manus.im/) highlights creating slides, websites, games, video, and design work. Its official site also lists research, browser operation, a Slack integration, an API, and a team offering. It is a relevant delegated-work alternative; this comparison does not assume its scope stops at individual use.

## Compare at a glance

| Criterion | Tale | Manus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Documented scope | Project tasks for research, documents and software with configured agents. | Research and browser work alongside slides, websites, games, video and design. |
| Team work | Task ownership, discussions, shared files and deliverable review. | A team offering with Slack and API entry points. |
| Outputs to validate | Confirm the tools and formats needed for each requested deliverable. | Try the required creation workflow and assess its returned artifacts. |

## Define the part of the job you are buying

Consider Manus when your main requirement is giving an agent a brief and working with the artifact it produces. Test the output types you actually need and how your colleagues can inspect, revise, and reuse them. Include the team configuration relevant to your organization in that evaluation.

Consider Tale when the central requirement is a project shared by teammates and configured agents, with assigned work and review visible together. Its [project tasks](https://docs.tale.dev/platform/projects/tasks) provide the structure for ownership and progress. Agent outputs contribute to that shared process. Verify the required authoring tools and deliverable formats rather than assuming a project workspace automatically generates every kind of media.

## Evaluate a campaign with three deliverables

Use a fictional event brief requiring an audience research note, an announcement draft, and a presentation outline. Change the audience after the first draft and ask a colleague to reject one unsupported claim.

In both candidates, follow how the change reaches each deliverable and how a second teammate takes over unfinished work. Judge output usability and the clarity of responsibility separately. The result should help you decide whether artifact production or ongoing project coordination is the main need. [Request a Tale demo](https://tale.dev/request-demo) with this campaign pilot.
