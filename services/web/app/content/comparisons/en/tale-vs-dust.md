Tale and Dust both put people and agents in shared work, including assigned tasks. The useful comparison is how the task lifecycle, runtime choices, and deployment model fit your team. A project board alone does not separate these products.

## Two approaches to shared AI work

Dust connects company sources and tools to shared agents. Its Pods combine conversations, files, and tasks that people or agents can assign, start, and complete. Task-linked conversations let teammates follow and redirect work. This overlaps directly with Tale. [Explore Dust](https://dust.tt/) and [Pod tasks](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/user-documentation/pods/tasks).

Tale gives a team a project space for tasks, discussion, files, and agent assignments. A marketing lead can brief a campaign, delegate research and copy tasks, then inspect the outputs with teammates. Agents work in persistent sandbox workspaces; configured capacity determines how much runs concurrently. The board provides a place to track what still needs attention after an answer has been produced. See [project agents](https://docs.tale.dev/platform/projects/project-agents).

## Evaluate a campaign handoff

Use the same approved product brief, interview notes, and brand guidance in both trials. Ask for a campaign narrative and three audience-specific drafts. Then have a second teammate change one requirement after work has started.

Watch how the team finds the current brief, understands who owns each part, and requests a revision. Check the work's sources and the final files, not only the first response. Record the setup effort and the amount of context teammates need to repeat. This exercise tests the collaboration pattern you will use every week.

## Which fits your operating model?

Shortlist Dust if its shared agents, connected company context, and Pod task lifecycle fit your team. Shortlist Tale if its task coordination, persistent sandbox workspaces, and control over deployment and supported runtimes match your operating requirements. Compare these combinations with the same work; configure access and judge outputs in either case.
